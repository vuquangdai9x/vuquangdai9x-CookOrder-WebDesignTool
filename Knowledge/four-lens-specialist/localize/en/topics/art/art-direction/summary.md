# Art Direction Summary

**A working grammar of framing, light, and continuity.** Blain Brown's *Cinematography: Theory and Practice* treats every camera/light/color choice as rhetorical — it must justify its presence by adding meaning, not just look correct. Composition principles (headroom, noseroom, chiaroscuro and atmospheric depth cueing) give a checklist for virtual-camera framing and key-art crops. Lens length is an emotional lever independent of framing: wide-and-low reads mythic, long-and-flat reads intimate. The strongest single character-lighting rule — avoid flat on-axis front light, key from upstage, add backlight/kicker for silhouette separation — is a three-step fix for any character rig; the "back cross-key" setup is a reusable dialogue-scene template. The 180°/action-axis rule and "master first, then locked answering shots" apply directly to sequencer-based cutscene camera authoring. Its production-workflow "Page Turn" review (every department confirms readiness scene-by-scene in one meeting) is a reusable cross-discipline gate before locking a cutscene. → `../../../lookup/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md`
