# Designing for Passive Engagement — Lookup Summary

## Interaction and Agency Are Separate
Passive engagement occupies more than one design space: spectating has low interaction and no agency, idle or simulation play has infrequent interaction but meaningful agency, and on-rails experiences may demand frequent input with little influence. Plot the cadence of input separately from its impact. Each quadrant serves different motivations and needs different feedback.
→ see learned/project-horseshoe-2018-r04-designing-for-passive-engagement.md § Interaction and Agency Are Separate

## Why Players Watch
People watch to learn, root for someone, save time or money, follow curiosity, admire mastery, enjoy spectacle or commentary, share a cultural moment, relax, and take pride in a created system. Watchability grows when the design makes state, stakes, skill, personalities, and turning points legible. Spectator value often depends on framing around the game as much as mechanics inside it.
→ see learned/project-horseshoe-2018-r04-designing-for-passive-engagement.md § Why Players Watch

## Designing the Viewing Loop
Give viewers a readable baseline, uncertainty about resolution, and visible changes that reward attention. Burst-interaction games need clear forecasts and satisfying observation between decisions; esports need rivalries, identities, expertise, history, and community rituals. Design for the person who is not currently pressing buttons, including the player watching their own system unfold.
