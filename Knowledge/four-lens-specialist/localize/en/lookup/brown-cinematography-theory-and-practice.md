# Cinematography: Theory and Practice — Lookup Summary

## Writing with Motion (Visual Language of Cinematography)
Defines cinematic technique as the full set of non-verbal tools — frame, lens, light/color, texture, movement, establishing, and point-of-view — used to layer meaning onto a scene's literal content. Uses a scene-by-scene reading of *Blade Runner* to show that no visual element (a building style, a coat) should be arbitrary; every choice must be answerable to "why is this here." Establishes the six-part toolkit the rest of the book expands on.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Writing with Motion (Visual Language of Cinematography)

## Visual Language (Composition Principles)
Treats composition as information design: the frame guides the eye in a deliberate order rather than being perceived all at once. Covers classical design principles (unity, balance, rhythm, contrast, directionality), the depth cues used to fake three dimensions on a flat frame (overlap, relative size, linear perspective, chiaroscuro, atmospheric perspective), and practical people-framing rules (headroom, noseroom, rule of thirds).
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Visual Language (Composition Principles)

## Language of the Lens
Explains how focal length is an independent storytelling channel from framing: wide lenses expand/distort space (comedic or unsettling close up, more immersive at a distance), long lenses compress space and flatten movement, and both interact with depth-of-field to control what's legible in a shot. Covers focus as a deliberate storytelling tool (rack focus, selective focus, priority rules for who gets focus) and the psychological reads of lens height and angle (high angle diminishes, low angle intimidates, Dutch tilt disorients).
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Language of the Lens

## Visual Storytelling (Light as Metaphor)
Uses a sustained scene-by-scene analysis of *The Natural* to show lighting used as a consistent emotional through-line across an entire film — warm backlight for innocence, cold blue moonlight for temptation, slatted shadow for corruption. Traces this technique back to Baroque chiaroscuro painting and to film noir's low-key, morally ambiguous lighting style.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Visual Storytelling (Light as Metaphor)

## Coverage & Continuity
Lays out the professional shooting methods (master scene method, overlapping/triple-take method, in-one, freeform/documentary method) and the four types of continuity (content, movement, position, time). Derives the 180°/action-axis rule from first principles, including the legitimate ways to cross it (neutral-axis exit, visible camera move, true reverse), plus the 20%/30° rule for making two similar angles cuttable and a catalog of the six functional cut types.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Coverage & Continuity

## Color
Covers color as both perception (hue/value/saturation, warm/cool psychology, the counterintuitive Kelvin scale) and technical pipeline (color temperature, the independent magenta/green axis, CIE gamut, Rec.709/Rec.2020/DCI-P3/ACES). Explains the three gel families (conversion, light-balancing, color-compensating) and the ASC-CDL system for portably communicating a cinematographer's color intent through post.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Color

## Cameras, Sensors, Measurement & Exposure
Covers the digital signal path (RAW vs. baked-in, Bayer demosaicing, rolling vs. global shutter), sensor size's role in depth-of-field independent of resolution, ISO as a noise/dynamic-range tradeoff, and the waveform monitor/vectorscope (especially RGB parade view) as the ground truth for exposure and color balance over an uncalibrated preview monitor.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Cameras, Sensors, Measurement & Exposure

## Lighting — Tools, Basics, and Technique
Defines the manipulable attributes of light (hard/soft, direction, color, intensity, texture) and the standard lighting vocabulary (key, fill, backlight, kicker, practicals). States the core doctrine that flat, on-axis front lighting is almost always wrong, and that lighting from the upstage side plus backlight/kicker is the default fix. Covers the back cross-key two-person dialogue setup and the high-key/low-key contrast-ratio choice for tonal register.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Lighting — Tools, Basics, and Technique

## Optics & Focus
Decomposes depth-of-field into its real drivers (focal length, aperture, focus distance, sensor size) and corrects the common myth that wide lenses inherently give more depth-of-field in practice. Covers nodal-point mechanics relevant to VFX plate/motion-control rigs and selective-focus tools (diopters, tilt/swing macro mounts) for manipulating the focal plane.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Optics & Focus

## Camera Movement
Frames every camera move as rhetorical, requiring motivation (character action or new composed information) and a settle beat before any cut. Catalogs move types and rigs (dolly, crane, Steadicam, drones, motion control) and highlights the reverse-zoom/dolly-zoom ("Hitchcock zoom") as a classic subjective-disorientation effect.
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Camera Movement

## Set Operations & Production Workflow
Documents the human pipeline from DP/director conversations through department-head equipment lists to the Page Turn production meeting, where every department confirms shot-by-shot readiness. Details the strict division of on-set camera-crew responsibility: operator (framing/moves), First AC (focus/exposure/gate checks), Second AC (slating/media), and DIT (color pipeline).
→ see learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Set Operations & Production Workflow

## Key Takeaways for Game Art Direction & Cutscene Lighting
- Justify every lens, framing, and lighting choice as a deliberate device, using the *Blade Runner*/*Punch-Drunk Love* model → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Writing with Motion (Visual Language of Cinematography)
- Apply headroom/noseroom/rule-of-thirds as a baseline checklist for virtual cameras and key-art crops → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Visual Language (Composition Principles)
- Use simulated lens length (FOV) as a primary emotional lever on virtual cameras, and budget depth-of-field for hero cutscene beats → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Language of the Lens
- Build a sustained lighting motif per character/faction across a whole narrative arc rather than lighting each cutscene in isolation → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Visual Storytelling (Light as Metaphor)
- Respect the 180°/action-axis rule in cutscene camera rigs, and author cameras master-first with overlap past the intended cut point → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Coverage & Continuity
- Grade cutscenes and key art on separate warm/cool and magenta/green axes, and keep a portable numeric look description across the engine-to-marketing pipeline → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Color
- Default character-lighting rule: avoid flat front lighting, key from the upstage side, add backlight/kicker for separation → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Lighting — Tools, Basics, and Technique
- Reuse the back cross-key setup for over-the-shoulder dialogue cutscenes, and use high-key/low-key contrast ratio to encode tonal register → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Lighting — Tools, Basics, and Technique
- Every cutscene camera move should be motivated and settle before a cut; reserve the dolly-zoom effect for reveal/dread beats → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Camera Movement
- Run a cross-department "page turn" review before locking a cutscene so animation, lighting, and grade are checked together → learned/brown-cinematography-theory-and-practice.md § Set Operations & Production Workflow
