# Designing Feelings of Companionship with NPCs — Learned Notes

## Five Companion Factors

Personhood is the impression of an autonomous individual with motives and an inner life. Bonding grows through memorable shared experience, vulnerability, trust, and relationship choices. Investment is time, attention, customization, or resources spent on the relationship. Value is a distinctive gameplay benefit. Stakes mean action or neglect can change or end the bond.

These are design lenses, not a checklist to maximize. XCOM units can create attachment through value and permanent stakes despite limited personhood; a richly realized story companion may work chiefly through personhood.

## Strong Predictors and Failure Modes

In the report's exploratory survey, personhood was very predictive, value and stakes strong, bonding moderate, and investment weak. The lesson is to build a few dimensions deeply and validate them, rather than spreading effort across every category.

Repetitive lines, broken AI, menu talk, unexplained control, forgotten actions, and replaceability expose the machinery. A companion that blocks progress or demands too much attention converts value into liability. Investment alone may create sunk cost without companionship.

## Designing Attachment

Signal life beyond the player's journey: private goals, relationships, opinions, environmental interaction, and selective availability. Build bonding through shared adversity, trust tests, repair, and player-influenced moments. Provide useful synergy without making the companion a mere tool. Credible departure, betrayal, injury, or death gives the relationship weight, but should not become arbitrary punishment.

Research should distinguish liking, attachment, importance, utility, frustration, and companionship. Test close to the experience to reduce memory and reputation bias.

### Companion Audit

- Chosen high-strength factors
- Independent motives and world life
- Shared experience and trust
- Distinctive utility without liability
- Consequences and loss
- Behavior that exposes the system
- Evidence from player research
