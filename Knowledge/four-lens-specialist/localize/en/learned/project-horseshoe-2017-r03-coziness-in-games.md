# Coziness in Games — Learned Notes

## Safety Abundance and Softness

Coziness is a designed emotional condition, not a synonym for cute art. It combines safety, abundance, and softness. Safety means no immediate threat, coercion, or punishment for honest expression. Abundance means urgent material needs are handled, freeing attention for connection and growth. Softness communicates low stress through manageable scope, gentle stimuli, intimacy, sincerity, and slower tempo.

The three dimensions are diagnostic. A beautiful room is not cozy if a timer is expiring; abundant resources do not help if strangers can intrude; a safe mechanic can still feel harsh when feedback is loud and invasive.

## Protecting the Cozy State

Any mechanic that manufactures an urgent need can collapse coziness. Common offenders include expiring rewards, mandatory upkeep, needy dependents, surprise notifications, social obligation, betrayal, confinement, and high-intensity audiovisual effects. Familiarity helps only when it is trustworthy.

Contrast is useful when boundaries are credible. Rain beyond a window, danger beyond a town wall, or combat outside a sanctuary can intensify relief. The same element crossing the boundary turns refuge into threat. Designers should therefore test not only whether a safe space exists, but whether players believe it will remain safe.

## Cozy Pacing and Practice

Coziness supports a rest-and-adventure rhythm. A low-pressure location, trusted NPC, or quiet maintenance activity lets players regulate arousal without leaving the game. That control can broaden appeal and sustain engagement while creating room for social and emotional practice.

Consent is the ethical foundation. Intimacy must be optional, exit must be easy, and power differences must be acknowledged. A pleasant surface that hides stakes or discourages disagreement is not psychological safety.

### Cozy Design Audit

- Which threats, shortages, and obligations are absent?
- Which signals communicate softness?
- Can the player opt in, pause, and leave?
- What can unexpectedly seize attention?
- Is the safe boundary reliable?
- Can people disagree without losing belonging?
