# Designing for Passive Engagement — Learned Notes

## Interaction and Agency Are Separate

Two axes clarify passive play. Interaction cadence ranges from no input through occasional bursts to constant control. Agency ranges from negligible influence to strong control over outcome. Spectating and recorded viewing sit low on both; idle games and simulations combine sparse input with consequential decisions; slots and many linear narratives can combine input with little outcome control.

Do not assume fewer inputs mean less engagement. The player may form predictions, inspect change, or emotionally invest while the system runs.

## Why Players Watch

Viewer motivations include learning, rooting interest, shortcutting, curiosity, admiration of expertise, spectacle, cultural context, commentary, pride in one's creation, surprise, fantasy, familiarity, and relaxation. A feature should name which motivation it serves and how the viewer receives evidence of value.

Spectators need readable identity and stakes. Team colors, personalities, rivalries, history, community membership, and expert commentary turn an opaque match into a human story. Simulation watchers need observable consequence and a sense that their earlier choices continue to matter.

## Designing the Viewing Loop

A watchable loop establishes comprehensible state, poses an uncertain question, signals progress, and lands a visible resolution. Camera, interface, pacing, replays, commentary hooks, and audio can reveal causality without requiring expert input.

For burst play, preview what will happen and make the passive interval informative rather than dead time. For streams, allow distinct runs and shareable surprises so watching does not consume all value of playing.

### Passive Engagement Audit

- Interaction cadence and agency level
- Primary viewer motivation
- Readable actors, goals, and stakes
- Prediction and resolution loop
- Evidence of mastery and causality
- Value retained for later active play
